# IvyLevel

IvyLevel (also referred to as Ivy Mentors and Ivy Specialists in earlier stages) was a college admissions mentorship startup founded by Siraj Nazir. Maddie Wang served as an advisor and early collaborator from May 2023 through early 2024. The company targeted high school students and their parents seeking college admissions guidance, particularly families of South Asian, East Asian, and international backgrounds aiming for selective US universities.

## Background and founder

Siraj Nazir (+14155319221) is a Bay Area entrepreneur who had previously worked in fintech and foodtech before pivoting to edtech. His LinkedIn handle "sirajnazir" appears in URLs shared in the IvyLevel Board chat in December 2023. He described the startup publicly as growing from fintech to foodtech to edtech. Nazir has a daughter, Huda, whose own academic journey informed his vision for the company. He described IvyLevel as "a FEMINIST Dad's startup" motivated by his desire to support girls in STEM who faced social barriers.

## Business model

IvyLevel offered personalized college admissions mentorship, connecting high school students with Stanford-affiliated mentors. The core value proposition evolved over the course of 2023 from a general mentorship model toward what Nazir called a "micro-services" approach: bounded, outcome-specific sessions rather than long-term packages.

Documented service types included essay editing, extracurricular strategy, chance assessments, FAFSA guidance, and SAT preparation critique. Nazir argued that SAT over-preparation was a common mistake among Asian immigrant families and positioned IvyLevel to offer alternative guidance. Pricing in early 2024 included one-year programs priced at approximately $10,800 and two-year programs at $17,280.

Named mentors included Janice (an early mentor with a track record of 7 successful Stanford applicants), Jenny (a Stanford CS freshman who joined as PM intern), and Erin (a marketing intern). Maddie was referenced as a key advisor whose perspective shaped how Nazir thought about product and pricing.

## Maddie's role

Maddie joined the project as an advisor in May 2023. Her responsibilities included LinkedIn content strategy, product feedback, and lead generation outreach. Nazir described her role as a "cofounder" in at least one message to a third party, though the formal structure is not documented.

Her contributions included reviewing marketing materials and Figma mockups, advising on pricing (strongly advocating for small, low-risk entry-point sessions over multi-session bundles), writing or advising on LinkedIn posts, and facilitating introductions to potential mentors and customers. She attended meetings with Nazir in person, and coordinated calls via Stanford Zoom links.

Maddie also participated in the IvyLevel Board group chat (chat 1752), which included Siraj Nazir and Jenny (+19717245148). The board chat was used to share customer insights, LinkedIn content topics, and product strategy updates.

## Marketing strategy

IvyLevel's primary acquisition channel was LinkedIn, where Nazir posted content targeting Bay Area parents of high school students. By January 2024, Nazir reported strong traction on LinkedIn posts mentioning middle school students and immigrant parent journeys. Maddie proposed a complementary strategy using Slack, Discord, Reddit, and Facebook parent groups, noting that those channels had driven 94 conversions for her own Founders Cafe project compared to 13 from LinkedIn.

Nazir's LinkedIn posts frequently drew on personal narratives, including stories about Huda's experience being bullied by male peers in STEM and her subsequent CS award win. He described these posts as resonating with both South Asian and East Asian parent audiences.

## End of collaboration

The collaboration ended in February 2024 following a disagreement. Nazir sent Maddie a message on February 17, 2024, referencing a December 2023 text in which she had acknowledged limited bandwidth and suggesting she had never been fully committed. He cited a $1,000 upfront payment as a "gesture of trust" and said she had been "over paid in many occasions." Maddie responded requesting space and a pause on communication. Nazir's final message suggested reconciliation was possible in the future.

## Name evolution

The company's name changed several times. Messages reference "Ivy Mentors" and "Ivy Specialists" before settling on "IvyLevel." Nazir traced an earlier version called "Gigzbee" and noted he had come back to that original concept after learning from customers.
