# 3 Stanford Queer Girls

"3 Stanford Queer Girls" was the name of an iMessage group chat connecting Maddie Wang, [[people/ngoc]], and [[people/lainey]] during and after their time at Stanford University. The chat was active from at least April 2022 through 2024 and served as a practical coordination channel for housing, social events, logistics, and mutual support. The group chat identifier is chat_id 1183, with participants including phone numbers +16504401196 (Ngoc) and +18329864330 (Lainey). The name was user-set and reflects the group's shared identity at the time of formation.

## Origins and Housing Search

The earliest recorded activity in the group (April 2022) shows the three women coordinating a shared housing search in San Francisco. They searched listings on Craigslist, toured apartments, and negotiated with landlords. Maddie coordinated the financial side, wiring deposits and tracking payments in Splitwise. Ngoc preferred listings with in-unit laundry and was willing to pay $100 more per month for it. The group eventually moved into an apartment at 1540 Fell Street in San Francisco, which they rented informally from an older landlord and split three ways: Maddie and Lainey together, and Ngoc separately.

Prior to move-in, the three met at Munger Hall on Stanford campus to stage and load belongings, with Maddie coordinating car logistics. They also toured together and weighed backup options including a Daly City listing at $2,500 for a two-bedroom.

## Life at 1540 Fell Street

After moving in during June 2022, the group chat functioned as a household coordination channel. Topics included parking permits, grocery logistics, gym recommendations, laundry access, paper towels, and COVID exposure protocols. In June 2022, Ngoc notified the group that she had been exposed to COVID at her workplace (where she was awaiting OPT approval to start working), and communicated her isolation plan in detail. Other members responded with offers of rapid tests and care.

Ngoc's youngest sister stayed briefly at the apartment during a period of political instability in Vietnam. The group navigated this informally and supportively.

## Social Events

The group chat was used to organize social outings. Maddie invited Ngoc and Lainey to Electroluxx, a queer Pride party in San Francisco, in May 2022. She shared an Instagram link and offered to get tickets. The group also coordinated dim sum outings in San Francisco, including a trip to Pho Huong Viet after plans changed. Lainey had attended a concert for which Ngoc held tickets. The group made plans around music, food, and shared activities throughout summer 2022.

## Identity and Significance

The group name "3 Stanford Queer Girls" reflected a shared identity at a specific intersection: queer women who had met at Stanford. The chat provided a space where that identity was assumed and did not need to be explained. Maddie used the group to coordinate practical life matters during a transition period out of dormitory housing. She directed messages to Ngoc and Lainey by name within the group, distinguishing their roles: Ngoc as a reliable communicator and logistics partner, Lainey as co-tenant and romantic partner.

Maddie described Ngoc's communication style warmly in a May 2022 message to the group: "I don't know anyone w better communication skills than u Ngoc ur amazing." The three navigated the typical friction of co-living alongside the intensity of a post-Stanford transition summer in San Francisco.

## Duration and Continuation

Activity in the group chat is documented through at least mid-2024. By that point, Lainey had moved to New York and Ngoc's housing situation had changed. The chat served its most intensive function during the June-September 2022 shared living period.
