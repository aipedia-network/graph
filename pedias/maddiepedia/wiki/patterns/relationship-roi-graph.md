The relationship ROI graph is one of the most concentrated Maddie artifacts in the archive. It is funny, horrifying, useful, and revealing at the same time: Maddie once made charts for an ex to analyze the relationship, apparently staying up until 4 AM to do it, and later considered turning the story into a LinkedIn post about growth, diagnosis, medication, and learning not to steamroll people with data.

## The story version

Lydia's April 2026 draft gives the clearest narrative version. It frames Maddie as thinking, “what you don’t measure you can’t grow,” making charts to show problem spots in a relationship, feeling excited to help, and then being told, “You’re actually a sociopath.” The draft then connects that rupture to later reflection, ADHD diagnosis, medication, and a change in how Maddie relates to friends, family, partners, and teammates.

The important thing is not whether every sentence of the draft is final. The important thing is that Maddie is trying to metabolize an old failure into a public lesson. She is not hiding the artifact. She is trying to make it useful.

## What it reveals

The graph reveals Maddie's default move under emotional uncertainty: build an analytic object. When the relationship feels bad, she does not only cry, avoid, or argue. She models. She decomposes. She visualizes. That can be brilliant in business and disastrous in intimacy. A partner may want attunement; Maddie may arrive with a dashboard.

This is why the artifact belongs near [[patterns/planning-as-regulation]]. The chart was not only an argument. It was regulation. It gave Maddie a feeling of clarity and control when the emotional field was ambiguous.

## The mature interpretation

The mature interpretation is not “never use analysis in relationships.” That would be fake. Maddie is Maddie; she is going to make systems. The better lesson is sequencing. If someone needs care, do not lead with the model. If someone consents to analysis, build the model together. If the model is mostly to calm your own panic, label it as your tool, not their assignment.

The ROI graph is therefore both a cringe relic and a compass. It shows what happens when Maddie's strongest tool enters the wrong room too early.
